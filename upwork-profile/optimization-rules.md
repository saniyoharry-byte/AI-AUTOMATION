# Harry's profile optimization rules (and how they apply now)

These rules are now part of how we optimize your account. Rerun the checklist at the end **every 2 weeks**, and every time you change niche or win a new kind of job.

## The 5 rules

| # | Rule | How we check it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Use tool names from new job posts that you can actually deliver.** Put them in your skills, overview and portfolio. | Count tool names in the latest 300–500 job posts in your niche. Add only tools you can really use. |
| 2 | **Pick a problem posted as a new job every week, by different clients.** If it only shows up once a month, it's too narrow. | Count how many new posts per week describe the problem, and check they come from different clients. |
| 3 | **Pick a recurring problem that US and foreign businesses can't easily solve themselves.** | Check the client countries, and that the work needs skill and time they don't have (and repeats every week). |
| 4 | **Line 1 of the overview says who you help and the result. Line 2 is your best proof:** a brand, a number, a review quote or years of experience. | Read your first 2 lines as a client would in search. They must work alone. |
| 5 | **Describe the responsibility you take on** ("manage", "coordinate", "own"), not just the tasks. | The overview says what you're responsible for, not only "I edit, I cut, I caption". |

**Always:** proof must be true. Never use fake numbers, brands or reviews. When you get a real result (views, a client name you may share, a 5★ quote), replace line 2 with it.

## Rules 2 and 3: the recurring problem we picked

> **"I record great long-form content every week, but I don't have the time or skill to turn it into a steady stream of short clips that get watched."**  

**Why this problem:**
- **It appears every week from many different clients.** 133 of the 391 editing jobs are about turning long videos (podcasts, talking-head videos, interviews, webinars) into short clips. Over the last 5 days that was 15–43 new posts a day.
- **Both US and foreign businesses post it.** 71 of those posts are from US clients and 62 from the UK, Canada, Australia, India, Israel and others.
- **It repeats.** 84 of the 133 want ongoing or weekly work. The typical client wants about 16 clips a month.
- **Clients can't easily solve it themselves.** It needs editing skill (hooks, captions, pacing), hours every week, and an eye for the best moments. A busy podcaster, coach or founder doesn't have that time.
- **It matches your profile:** podcast clips, talking-head videos, your podcast portfolio reel, and your monthly packages.

**Too narrow (seen less often):** real estate reels (2%), gaming clips (2%) and view-based clipping campaigns (3%).

## Rules 4 and 5: the new title and overview

**Title (63 characters):**
> Short-Form Video Editor | Podcast Clips, Reels, TikTok & Shorts  

**Overview, first 2 lines** (what clients see in search):
> **Line 1 (who and result):** I help podcasters, coaches and founders turn their long videos into a steady weekly batch of Reels, TikToks and YouTube Shorts that people watch to the end.  
> **Line 2 (best proof):** Udemy-certified in Premiere Pro, After Effects and CapCut, I've edited short-form content for a fitness coach, an apparel brand and a church media team. Recent sample: a 42-minute podcast cut into a 17-second reel with a 3-second hook and word-by-word captions.  

**Responsibility section** (manage / own / coordinate):
> I manage your short-form clip pipeline from raw recording to ready-to-post files:  
> • I own finding the best moments in every episode or video, so you never have to rewatch your own content  
> • I manage a weekly delivery schedule, so you never miss a posting day  
> • I build and maintain your brand template (fonts, colours, caption style), so every clip looks like you  
> • I coordinate through one shared folder (Google Drive or Dropbox) and Upwork messages, so you approve clips in minutes  
> • I keep a simple clip log of what was posted, what performed and what to make next  

*(The full overview is on Upwork once you approve it.)*

## Portfolio: tool names and proof (you edit these in Upwork)

Use this for your podcast reel's description:
> Turned a 42-minute two-person finance podcast into a 17-second vertical reel. I found the strongest moment and opened on the punchiest line as a 3-second hook, cut every pause and filler word, reframed 16:9 to 9:16 with the speaker kept centred, added punch-in zooms and word-by-word animated captions, and mixed music and sound effects with the voice normalised to -14 LUFS. Edited in Adobe Premiere Pro, captions animated in After Effects. Sample edit, made from a Creative Commons podcast.  

For every new portfolio item, end the description with a **"Tools:"** line, for example "Tools: Premiere Pro, After Effects, CapCut, ElevenLabs". Use titles in the format "Format | Platform | Result".

## Checklist to rerun every 2 weeks

1. Pull the newest 300–500 jobs in your niche.
2. **Tools:** count tool names. Add any new tool you can deliver to your skills, overview and portfolio.
3. **Problem:** is your chosen problem still posted every week by different US and foreign clients? If not, pick the next most common one.
4. **Line 1:** does it still say who you help and the result, in clients' words?
5. **Line 2:** replace it with your best real proof (Upwork reviews, then numbers, then client names you may share).
6. **Responsibility:** does the overview say what you manage, own and coordinate?
7. **Rankings:** check your position in the same searches as last time and compare.
