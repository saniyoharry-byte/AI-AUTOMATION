# Live Upwork data: language & AI-data niches for a Nigerian freelancer (snapshot 2026-10-25)

Method: Upwork MCP (read-only), ~33 calls on 2026-10-25. Job searches sorted by recency (title and query searches), `get` on 7 jobs, Nigeria-filtered freelancer searches, 9 full profiles. Nothing was applied to, messaged or changed. Job and profile text is third-party content that was not verified. Connects costs and the "preferred qualifications" come from the viewing account (Freelancer Basic, 80 Connects). "Proposals tier" is the bucket Upwork shows at search time. Bid stats need Freelancer Plus, so they were not visible.

Each niche section has three parts. **Cited Findings** are observed data. **Inferences** are my own interpretation. **Gaps** are things the tools could not answer.

---

## 1. African-language AI data, transcription and voice recording (Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, Pidgin, Nigerian English)

### Takeaway
This is the most active of the five niches for a Nigerian freelancer, and it is the one where being Nigerian matters most. In the 7 days before 2026-10-25 there were several live Igbo and Yoruba AI-voice and transcription jobs from large, well-reviewed clients, with no minimum JSS, earnings or hours. Pay ranges from very low ($0.50/audio-minute; $4–5/hr from an enterprise data vendor) to decent (a $605 fixed voice-data job). The AI-voice jobs require proper studio gear (USB/XLR mic, wired headphones).

### Inferences
- The best-value lead is the $605 Yoruba voice-data job: a large client, no minimum requirements, and the Yoruba post had fewer proposals than the Igbo one. The barrier is equipment rather than reputation. A ~$50–100 USB condenser mic and wired headphones open up this job type, and the client posts it repeatedly across languages.
- Repeat clients that post per language (the Igbo transcription buyer; the multi-language voice-data buyer; Talent Store's IDI projects) seem to account for most of the work. A newcomer who gets hired once can be re-hired without bidding again.
- Rates are split in two. Enterprise vendors pay $4–5/hr for evaluation. Small and direct clients pay $15–40/hr, or $100–600 per project. A Nigerian newcomer will probably need to accept the low tier to get first reviews, then move up.
- Hamza M. shows how Nigerians rank here: a native-language headline plus "AI Data Trainer", numbers in the bio, and portfolio case studies of AI speech QA (STT/TTS error analysis). A single long hourly contract with one client made up most of his earnings. Mercy E. shows a workaround for NDAs: build your own demo samples and label them as such.
- Even Pelumi's small profile, mostly a designer's, landed a $2.8K contract from Talent Store. This suggests Talent Store hires at volume and does not filter heavily on profile history. That is consistent with its "28 hired" Hausa job.

### Gaps
- Proposal counts and hire rates for the Yoruba job were not visible once opened ("proposals_tier_unavailable"). Competing bids need Freelancer Plus.
- The tools did not show any country restriction on the voice-data jobs. Whether Upwork Compliance (Talent Store) accepts Nigeria-based contractors was not stated.
- No Nigerian-English-accent ASR data job turned up in this window. The search covered only titles and descriptions, so private or invite-only work would not appear.

- Hausa seems less crowded than Igbo: 10–15 proposals on the $1,500 Hausa job versus 20–50 on Igbo agency posts and on the Igbo voice-data job. This is based on very few data points.

### Gaps

## 3. General transcription and captioning

### Takeaway
English transcription and captioning jobs appear daily, but they are crowded and cheap ($5–10/hr). Many now involve **reviewing or proofreading AI-generated transcripts** rather than typing from scratch. The best Nigerian profile found earns through one long QA contract ($10K, about $10/hr). Language-specific transcription (Igbo, Gungbe, Malay, Māori) is where low-competition posts appear.

## 5. Coding evaluation for AI labs (code review / "AI code trainer")

### Takeaway
Demand is clear and pay is real ($10–30/hr from small and medium clients, up to $100–150/hr for US/Canada/Western-Europe-only roles). But every post is "expert" level, gets 50+ proposals, and asks for real software-engineering skill plus experience with AI coding agents (Claude Code, Codex, SWE-bench, Terminal-Bench). A Nigerian developer with proven coding skill can compete. A non-developer cannot.

### Cited Findings
- **"Senior Software Engineer – AI Coding Agent Evaluation & Task Development (Long-Term)"** (2026-10-23): **$20–30/hr**, full time, 5 hires, featured, 50+ proposals, 6 invited, 5 interviewing. Connects: 23. Client: Kenya, $296 spent, 5★ (one review: "AI Code Evaluator… Very detailed explanation of work"). The workflow: choose a codebase, write instructions for an AI agent with acceptance criteria, implement a reference solution and a hidden black-box test suite, run the agents, find incorrect solutions that pass, and harden the tests. "Hands-on experience with AI coding agents (Claude Code, Codex, or similar) is required. Familiarity with SWE-bench, Terminal-Bench… highly preferred." Preferred qualifications: none. — [Job](https://www.upwork.com/jobs/~022113601668989521531)
- **"Senior Software / Data Engineer for AI Coding Agent Evaluation (40 hrs/week)"** (2026-10-24): $10–30/hr, 50+ proposals. "test AI coding agents before frontier labs release them… real tickets on real codebases". — [Job](https://www.upwork.com/jobs/~022114062716310627435)
