# Live Upwork data: media and 3D niches for a no-badge, zero-job newcomer

Scope: (1) 2D animation / explainer, (2) motion graphics (After Effects), (3) 3D modeling / Blender (non-Roblox), (4) podcast editing / audio, (5) YouTube automation / faceless scripts and voiceover, (6) AI image generation.
Method: a read-only pull through the Upwork MCP connector on **2026-10-25**. The newest job timestamps are around 23:49 UTC on Oct 25. Job searches used title keywords sorted by recency, 10 newest per term: "2D animation", "explainer video", "motion graphics", "Blender", "3D model", "podcast", "faceless", "YouTube automation", "AI image", "Midjourney". There were six freelancer searches (top 25 each) and eleven full profile pulls. One pull hit a PERMISSION throttle error and succeeded on retry. Same caveats as the Oct 24 report. Matching is by title only, so volumes are lower bounds. "No badge" means no Top Rated, Top Rated Plus or Expert-Vetted, because Rising Talent is not exposed. The search cards' completed-job counts were wrong again: Muhammad S. showed 1 and has 66 on his profile, Femi C. showed 1 and has 38, Adam A. showed 1 and has 26, Iqra A. showed 1 and has 12. So "newcomer" claims below rest on contract dates in the full profiles. All job and profile text is self-written by third parties and unverified.

## Q1. Demand and competition per niche (job side)

### Takeaway
Every niche posts several jobs a day, but competition varies sharply. **2D/stickman animation for YouTube** and **Blender product/asset work** had the most low-proposal posts. **Motion graphics, podcast editing and AI image** posts mostly drew 20–50 proposals. **YouTube automation** posts cluster at 5–15 proposals, but they come from a small set of templated "YouTube Automation company" clients who ask for "experienced" people.

### Inferences
- The lowest-competition posts in this pull were **long-running YouTube animation work at low prices** (the $30 stick-figure videos) and **narrow, technical Blender product jobs** (sunglasses try-on, the character rig for gym Shorts). Both had fewer than 5 proposals. By comparison, on Oct 24 AI UGC had 2/20 posts at 50+ and appointment setting had 13/20 under 5. Blender sits between the two; motion graphics and podcast editing look more like logo design, with most posts at 20–50 proposals.
- On a per-hour basis, YouTube animation and editing budgets are poor. Fifteen minutes of animation for $30, or 3-hour documentaries at $15–20/hr, put effective pay at or below the $3–7/hr admin band from the Oct 24 report.
- The "YouTube Automation company" burst looks like a few operators mass-posting templates. The volume is real, but the number of distinct buyers is smaller than the post count suggests.
- AI image demand by title is thin, about 2 genuine posts a day. Most AI-image money appears inside "AI UGC video" and "e-commerce product image" posts, which the Oct 24 report already covered. AI image does not look like a standalone niche.
- The duplicated Everfit brief, posted again by an account with $96 spent, and the $5 Revit job look like a reposting or scam pattern (Inference, not verified). The same goes for clients with many posts and almost no spend, such as the 83-post / $135 voiceover client. Avoid them.

### Gaps
- Posting volume beyond 10 posts per term was not sampled, and the timestamps cover one weekend (Oct 24–25). Weekday volume is unknown.
- The "podcast" search mostly returned video-podcast editing. Audio-only podcast editing volume was not isolated; a separate "audio editing" search was not run.
- Hire outcomes, invite counts and proposal texts are not visible.

### Inferences
- **What winning newcomers share.** Each has a narrow buyer-type title (SaaS explainer, faceless/cash-cow editing, AI apparel catalogue, Blender + Python automation). Each has a portfolio organized by the buyer's sub-niche. Each has repeat clients who send per-unit work ($50 audio edits, $80 biomes, $80–150 videos). This matches the Oct 24 finding that repeat hires drove most newcomer ladders.
- **Effective pay is low wherever the work is per video or per minute.** Stickman animation ran about $6/hr and faceless editing and AI tool testing about $8–10/hr. Higher pay showed up where the buyer is a business with a product: SaaS explainers at $200–500 each, Blender product and automation work at $80–450, and AI apparel catalogues at a $750 job. That matches the Oct 24 report's split between low-pay entry work and proof-gated work.
- **YING KIT C. is the closest analogue to Harry (Inference).** He is a no-badge 2026 account using Claude Code and Python to automate Blender and ComfyUI. He ranks on page 1, won 43 jobs and charges $45/hr, because his proof is engineering-grade rather than artistic. "Blender + Python automation / AI rendering pipelines" is a coder's side door into 3D that does not require being a top artist.
- **YouTube scriptwriting has a clear Nigerian presence** (8 of the top 25), but every one of them is Top Rated. A new profile would compete against established Nigerian peers at $10–30/hr.
- Padding repeats the Oct 24 patterns: inflated experience claims (Iqra), keyword-dump overviews (Muhammad S., Ayusha H., Adam A.), portfolio pieces that may be others' work (the "BY ARIF" watermark, Substance asset-library renders), and claimed brand employers (Audible). ### Gaps

- Search rank factors (recency, keyword match, JSS) cannot be separated with this data.

### Takeaway
Ranked for a generic newcomer (Inference built on Q1–Q2 observations):
1. **Blender product renders and technical 3D**, best for someone who can script (Python or AI pipelines).
2. **2D explainers aimed at SaaS buyers**, not YouTube stickman content.
3. **YouTube faceless video editing.** Easy to enter and high volume, but about $8–10/hr.
4. **Podcast and audio editing**, including ElevenLabs and AI voice polishing. Low ticket ($15–65), with repeat episodes.
5. **AI image generation**, which works only as e-commerce catalogue or product imagery and is thin as a title niche.
6. **YouTube automation scripting.** Templated expert-only clients and a badged, partly Nigerian search field.
7. **Motion graphics (After Effects).** Most posts at 20–50 proposals and 23/25 badged in search.

### Inferences
- **Against the Oct 24 scoreboard:** none of these six beats AI UGC video or appointment setting on combined ease and volume. Blender/technical 3D and SaaS explainers would slot near Shopify fixes and Sheets/Apps Script (ranks 5–7): moderate volume, fewer bids per post than n8n, and $100–500 tickets. Faceless editing and podcast editing would sit with short-form video (rank 4) and the admin group on pay. AI image and YouTube automation scripting fall below logo design. Motion graphics falls near WordPress and GoHighLevel: busy but crowded.
- **For Harry (Nigerian CS graduate with Claude Code, n8n and Python skills):** the one media/3D opening that fits his edge is **"Blender/ComfyUI + Python pipeline automation"** (YING KIT C.'s model). It could be an add-on item in his automation profile: "automated product-image or render pipelines". It is not worth a switch. The other five niches are taste- or craft-led and pay $8–15/hr at entry, the same mismatch the Oct 24 report flagged for UGC and short-form video.
- Speed still matters. The fewer-than-5 proposal tiers appeared mainly on posts minutes to hours old, the same pattern as Oct 24.

### Gaps
- No Nigerian no-badge newcomer was found in any of these six niches. The Nigerian profiles in search (Adam A., Yemi E., Femi C. and seven other scriptwriters) were all badged.
- Earnings per profile are shown only as bands ($1K+, $2K+), so effective hourly rates are approximate.
- One snapshot cannot show trend. The "YouTube automation" burst may be weekend-specific.
