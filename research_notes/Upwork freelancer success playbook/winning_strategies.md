# Winning strategies: 0 jobs to steady work on Upwork (2025–2026)

**Method note:** Researched 2026-10-24. Every page fetch failed: upwork.com, support.upwork.com and community.upwork.com were refused by the egress proxy, and techpoint.africa, theigc.org and blogs.worldbank.org failed DNS. **All findings below come from search-result snippets, not full pages.** Where a snippet reports a third-party claim, it is labelled. Most of the numbers about proposals come from vendors that sell proposal tools (GigRadar, UpHunt, AIProposer, GigUp, UpworkAlerts). Treat them as correlational and self-interested. The only independent, peer-reviewed or field-experimental evidence is the academic work in section 3 (Pallais 2014; Ahmed, Tirmazee & Wu 2025). Context: a new Roblox developer from Nigeria with 0 jobs, an $18/hr rate and about 80 Connects. The 50-profile study in `/home/user/AI-AUTOMATION/research/roblox-freelancer-analysis.md` is used only as context.

## 1. Proposal best practices: length, preview lines, personalization, questions, Loom, timing, boosting, win-rate data

### Takeaway
The client first sees only the opening line(s) next to your photo, rate, badges and JSS. Upwork's own guidance makes the hook the most important part of the proposal. Vendor data, which is not independent, links three things to higher reply rates: short tailored proposals, an offer to answer questions or record a short Loom, and applying early to jobs with few proposals. Expect a 2–10% win rate on your first ~30 proposals. Upwork has published no win-rate data that I could find.

### Cited Findings
**Preview / first lines**
- Upwork's 2026 cover-letter guide says clients first see the opening lines in the results list, alongside name, photo, talent badges, hourly rate and JSS, and then decide whether to open the full proposal. — [Upwork guide](https://www.upwork.com/guides/resumes-and-cover-letter) (snippet)
- An Upwork Community member says clients see "only the first two lines of your cover letter". — [Upwork Community](https://community.upwork.com/t5/Freelancers/How-does-our-proposals-show-to-the-client/td-p/1609801) (anecdote). Third-party estimates of the preview length vary: "2–3 lines", "~40 words" or "~150 characters". — [GigRadar](https://gigradar.io/blog/upwork-cover-letter-template); [Snipework](https://snipework.com/blog/upwork-cover-letter-sample) (unverified)
- Vendor claim: proposals that open with "I am a professional with X years of experience" convert at 1–3%. Proposals that open with the client's specific problem do better. — [Snipework](https://snipework.com/blog/upwork-cover-letter-sample) (unverified)

**Length and personalization**
- Vendor advice: 100–200 words, personalized to the job, with a portfolio link and one specific question. Also, "5–10 well-personalized proposals beats 30 generic ones". — [AIProposer win-rate guide](https://aiproposer.com/guides/upwork-proposals/upwork-proposal-win-rate) (vendor)
- Vendor claim: generic copy-paste proposals convert at 1–3% and personalized ones at 10–25%. — via [Skydo bidding guide](https://www.skydo.com/blog/upwork-bidding-guide) / [GigUp](https://giguphq.com/blog/win-rate-on-upwork-2026) (single vendor, unverified)
- Beginner guide: use at least one detail from the job post in every proposal, because "templates alone tend to get ignored". — [UpworkAlerts](https://upworkalerts.com/blog/upwork-tips-for-beginners) (vendor)

**Questions and Loom / video**
- GigRadar studied 133,872 proposals from its customers, Dec 2025–Feb 2026. The baseline reply rate was 7.45%. A closing line offering to "answer any questions you may have" was associated with about +5.89 percentage points, and offering a 1-minute Loom with about +3.70 points. — [GigRadar response-rate analysis](https://gigradar.io/blog/upwork-proposal-response-rate) (vendor, correlational, mostly agencies)
- GigRadar also studied 322 agency teams. Top-quartile teams (12.86% reply rate) mentioned Loom in 13.6% of proposals, against 7.6% in the bottom quartile (3.76% reply rate). Top teams also opened with a question far more often (14.2% vs 2.4%). — [GigRadar benchmarks](https://gigradar.io/blog/upwork-metrics-benchmarks-for-agencies) (vendor)
- UpHunt says video helps only when it is personalized to the job, and that a generic Loom can lower reply rates. — [UpHunt](https://uphunt.io/blog/upwork-video-proposals-loom-reply-rates-2026) (vendor; full table not visible in the snippet)

**Timing and competition**
- Vendor guidance: apply within the first few hours, and target jobs with fewer than 20 proposals. Avoid jobs posted more than 72 hours ago and jobs with more than 25 proposals. — [Skydo](https://www.skydo.com/blog/upwork-bidding-guide); [GigUp](https://giguphq.com/blog/win-rate-on-upwork-2026) (vendor). I found no Upwork-published data on the advantage of applying early.
- "Less than 5" proposals means you can still get in early. "50+" means you are fighting a crowd. — [Raenest](https://www.raenest.com/es-ar/blog/how-to-get-freelance-jobs-on-upwork) (blog)
- For new profiles, the "Best Matches" feed may surface better jobs than general search. — [Raenest](https://www.raenest.com/es-ar/blog/how-to-get-freelance-jobs-on-upwork) (blog claim)

**Boosted proposals (official mechanics)**
- Boosting is an auction. You bid extra Connects, on top of the normal submission cost, for the top slots on the client's proposal list. Winners are pinned near the top with a "Boosted" label. Eligibility depends on how strong a match Upwork judges you to be. A bid table shows what others are bidding. A boost lasts 7 days or until the client hires, and you can be outbid during that time. Connects are held, refunded when the auction closes, and then winners are charged the final amount. The client still decides whom to message or hire. — [Upwork Help: Boosted Proposals](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/4406541109011-Boosted-Proposals); [Upwork: How to boost](https://www.upwork.com/resources/how-to-boost-proposal) (snippets)
- Sources disagree on the number of slots: current pages say 4, and an older community announcement said 3. — [Upwork Community announcement](https://community.upwork.com/t5/Announcements/Boost-Your-Proposal-to-the-Top-of-List/m-p/957507)
- A LinkedIn post claims Upwork's AI shortlisting can rank non-boosted proposals higher. This is unverified. — [LinkedIn](https://pk.linkedin.com/in/shahnice)

**Win-rate benchmarks (all vendor, unverified)**
- New freelancers win 5–10% and experienced ones 15–25%. Expect 2–8% in your first 30 proposals. Most freelancers win 3–10%, and well-matched proposals get 20–40% responses. — [AIProposer](https://aiproposer.com/guides/upwork-proposals/upwork-proposal-win-rate); [GigUp](https://giguphq.com/blog/win-rate-on-upwork-2026); [AIProposer pricing](https://aiproposer.com/guides/upwork-proposals/proposal-pricing-strategy)
- A blog from April 2026 claims "17 of every 20 proposals receive no response", attributed to Upwork data. I could not trace the original source. — [tylerewillis.com](https://tylerewillis.com/blog/upwork-proposal-ignore-rate-platform-data) (unverified)

**AI-proposal saturation (context for 2025–26)**
- A blog says Upwork's Q1 2025 results reported 52% more users engaging with Uma, Upwork's AI assistant, than in Q4 2024, and argues this makes generic proposals look alike. — [vincentschmalbach.com](https://www.vincentschmalbach.com/why-no-one-reads-your-upwork-proposals-anymore/) (secondary characterization of an earnings report)
- Clients have started adding anti-AI disclaimers to job posts, and a hirer quoted by Forbes describes being "inundated" with generic, bland proposals. — [Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/news/innovation/chatgpt-writing-how-ai-is-upending-the-freelance-world/); [Upwork Community clients thread](https://community.upwork.com/t5/Clients/So-I-posted-a-job-this-happened/m-p/1433088/highlight/true)

### Inferences
- About 80 Connects buys only around 5–15 proposals, depending on per-job cost, so boosting is usually a poor use of a newcomer's Connects. The money is better spent on more early, well-matched, personalized proposals. Boost only rare, perfect-fit jobs where the bid table is cheap.
- For a Roblox developer, the vendor finding on short video maps naturally onto a 30–60 second screen capture of a working system: a gameplay loop, a DataStore save, a UI. Your strongest proof is a working demo, which the context study also found (Jay V.'s 11 short demo videos).
- Because AI-written proposals are now everywhere, a first line that quotes a specific mechanic from the job post and gives a concrete plan for it is probably what stands out most. This is inference.

### Gaps
- No official Upwork data on proposal win rates, how much applying early helps, or whether boosting increases the hire rate. The upwork.com pages could not be fetched; the snippet of the "how to boost" page showed no statistics.
- The GigRadar data is mostly agency teams and is correlational. It may not apply to solo newcomers.

## 2. Job selection for new freelancers: client signals, proposal counts, job age, scams, job size

### Takeaway
Apply only to clients with verified payment who have hired before and spent money, to jobs that are recent and have few proposals, and to small, well-scoped tasks. Turn down any request to move off-platform, to pay anything, to buy Connects or to share ID, voice or video. Upwork says it cannot recover money paid outside the platform.

### Cited Findings
- Payment Verified means the client added a valid payment method. "Do not apply to jobs without this. There is no payment protection if a client cannot pay." — [Raenest](https://www.raenest.com/es-ar/blog/how-to-get-freelance-jobs-on-upwork) (blog)
- Hire-rate thresholds differ by source: ">60%" or ">70%". Total spend of $10K+ suggests a serious client. Pair total spend with the average hourly rate the client pays. — [Raenest](https://www.raenest.com/es-ar/blog/how-to-get-freelance-jobs-on-upwork); [dev.to 5-signals](https://dev.to/umair_ulhassan_d200ebd9e/5-signals-framework-how-i-hit-100-job-success-on-upwork-5co7) (heuristics, not official)
- One freelancer's rule (the 100% JSS author on dev.to): for client accounts under 90 days old, require extra signals such as high spend or a good hire rate. For accounts under 30 days old, pass unless the job is extremely well defined. — [dev.to](https://dev.to/umair_ulhassan_d200ebd9e/5-signals-framework-how-i-hit-100-job-success-on-upwork-5co7) (anecdote)
- Sweet spot for a first contract: well-scoped, mid-budget jobs of about $200–$1,500 fixed price from clients with verified payment and a spending history. Do not chase the very biggest jobs or the very cheapest ones. — [UpworkAlerts beginner guide](https://upworkalerts.com/blog/upwork-tips-for-beginners) (vendor). On the forum, newcomers are told to start with smaller fixed-price jobs they can finish well. — [Upwork Community](https://community.upwork.com/t5/New-to-Upwork/New-to-upwork/m-p/586921) (anecdote)
- **Scams (official):** Upwork's Scams FAQ says it cannot track or verify payments made off-platform and "cannot assist with recovering payments made outside the platform". Always decline requests to take or make payments outside Upwork, and report them. — [Upwork Scams FAQ](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/34385274054547-Scams-Frequently-Asked-Questions)
- **Scam patterns:**
  - moving to Telegram, WhatsApp or email before a contract exists
  - asking you to pay for "training", "software" or a deposit
  - asking you to buy Connects so they can "officially hire" you
  - overpayment followed by a refund request
  - early requests for an ID or SSN, or for voice/video "verification" recordings that can be used for impersonation
  - malware files disguised as contracts
  - vague posts with unrealistic pay

  — [Upwork: spotting fake job posts](https://www.upwork.com/resources/spotting-fake-job-posts); [GigRadar scams](https://gigradar.io/blog/upwork-scams); [Skydo](https://cdn.skydo.com/blog/how-to-spot-scams-on-upwork); a forum case of a Telegram "secretary" offering $2,000 + $300 bonus for 5 articles in 3 days: [Upwork Community](https://community.upwork.com/t5/Support-Forum/Potential-Scammer/td-p/1525005)

### Inferences
- With limited Connects, a reasonable filter for a Roblox newcomer is all of the following:
  - payment verified
  - at least one past hire, or spend above $0
  - posted in the last 24 hours, ideally the last few hours
  - fewer than 10–15 proposals
  - a clear, small scope such as one system, a UI, a bug fix or a map section

  This combines the heuristics above and is not tested.
- Roblox has an unusually large number of young, first-time clients. Some are legitimate but inexperienced and some are scams. That makes client-history signals and on-platform-only rules especially important (inference).

### Gaps
- No reliable figure for the share of Upwork jobs that are scams. A source explicitly said such figures are not reliable.
- No data on which job sizes produce the most first reviews. The $200–$1,500 range is a vendor heuristic.

## 3. Pricing for newcomers: hourly vs fixed, low first-job pricing vs underpricing, raising rates

### Takeaway
The best evidence here is academic. In field experiments on large freelance platforms, new workers who were hired and reviewed went on to earn more and charge more (Pallais, AER 2014). In a 2025 study, new workers who bid lower than the posted budget got more visibility, more contacts and more hires, yet many novices wrongly believed a low bid signals poor quality. A temporary discount on the first few jobs is therefore supported by evidence. Raise prices after the first reviews.

### Cited Findings
- **Pallais (AER 2014, oDesk field experiment, 2010):** 3,767 data-entry applicants who bid ≤$3/hr took part, and 952 were randomly hired for 10-hour jobs and given public evaluations. Being hired, and getting detailed evaluations, "substantially improved later employment outcomes". Hired workers were more likely to be employed, asked for higher wages and earned more than controls. The author argues that new workers are hired less often than would be efficient, because a first job creates public information that the employer giving it does not capture. — [NBER w18917](https://www.nber.org/papers/w18917); [AEA RCT registry](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/1021); [Harvard page](https://pallais.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/inefficient-hiring-entry-level-labor-markets)
- **Ahmed, Tirmazee & Wu (2025, IGC policy brief Sept 2025; working paper on a large global freelancing platform):**
  - They describe a "reputation trap": you need ratings to get hired, but must be hired to earn ratings.
  - In a baseline survey of 481 novices, 44% believed that bidding below the posted budget signals low quality.
  - In the demand-side experiment, low wage offers "significantly improve hiring prospects". The IGC brief says lower offers raise both the visibility of applications and the chance of being contacted.
  - Giving workers accurate information made them more willing to lower their bids, and the gains were concentrated among *stronger* workers.
  - Without that information, workers learn slowly and at a cost.

  — [IGC publication](https://www.theigc.org/publications/cutting-wages-break-how-information-gaps-hold-back-workers-online-gig-economy); [IGC brief PDF](https://www.theigc.org/sites/default/files/2025-10/Ahmed-Tirmazee-Wu-Policy-Brief-September-2025.pdf); [World Bank Development Impact blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/impactevaluations/lower-prices--lower-chances--how-misbeliefs-keep-freelancers-out); [CUHK paper](https://econ.cuhk.edu.hk/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Paper_RuoxuanWu.pdf) (snippets only; exact effect sizes not seen; the paper is preliminary)
- Liang, Hong & Gu describe a "cold-start problem": employers mostly choose workers by existing reputation, which creates a high entry barrier. — [ISR 2025 via IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/orisre/v36y2025i1p600-620.html)
- Community views:
  - An Upwork Community expert says to price aggressively on early proposals "without undermining your credibility by overdoing it". — [Upwork Community](https://community.upwork.com/t5/Freelancers/Does-reducing-your-rates-as-a-new-member-give-you-an-advantage/td-p/1163805)
  - A guide suggests bidding at 70–80% of your target rate to start, and raising your rate after your first three positive reviews. — [UpworkAlerts](https://upworkalerts.com/blog/how-to-make-money-on-upwork) (vendor)
  - A blog argues the opposite: Upwork search may treat a very low rate as a quality signal and hide you from searches by experienced clients. — via [Raenest search snippet](https://www.raenest.com/es-ar/blog/how-to-get-freelance-jobs-on-upwork) (unverified)
- **Fixed vs hourly:** bidding a low fixed price lets you discount one job without lowering your public hourly rate. On hourly contracts, only the freelancer can raise their rate, and the change applies to future hours. Fixed-price contracts need new terms negotiated instead. — [Upwork Community](https://community.upwork.com/t5/New-to-Upwork/Will-initial-low-rate-hurt-me-later/td-p/568017); [Freelance with Erica](https://freelancewitherica.substack.com/p/hourly-vs-flat-fee-pricing-for-beginner-freelancers); [Upwork Help snippet](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/211062378-Manage-Job-Searches)
- Nigerian freelancers reportedly have the lowest rates on Upwork among five African countries, averaging $163 per job (Bookipi study as reported by BusinessDay; the study date is unclear). — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/life-arts/article/7-smart-ways-nigerians-are-earning-extra-income-online-in-2025/)
- Connects cost $0.15 each, reportedly unchanged through 2025–26. — [Snipework](https://snipework.com/blog/upwork-connects-cost-2026); [Upwork Help](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/211063228)

### Inferences
- For the $18/hr Roblox newcomer: keep the public hourly rate at $18, which sits in the range of earning Nigerian Roblox freelancers in the context study ($17–$20). Win the first 2–5 reviews with small fixed-price bids set somewhat below the client's budget. Then raise the rate, for example to $22–25, after about 3 good reviews. The academic evidence supports discounting *the bid*. It does not show that a permanently low profile rate helps.
- The 2025 finding that stronger workers gain most from bidding low suggests discounting works when paired with real proof, such as demos.

### Gaps
- Exact effect sizes from Ahmed et al. were not visible in the snippets, and the source pages could not be fetched.
- No evidence on whether Upwork's ranking penalizes low hourly rates. That claim is unverified.

## 4. First reviews ethically, turning first clients into repeat work, asking for feedback, JSS risk

### Takeaway
Feedback, public and private, is left when the contract closes, and private feedback weighs heavily in JSS. You can get a Rising Talent badge before you have a JSS. The ethical way to get a review is one polite message after delivery asking the client to close the contract, without asking for a specific rating. Fake or traded reviews break Upwork's terms. Repeat work comes from communicating often and anticipating the client's next need.

### Cited Findings
- JSS measures client satisfaction from contract history and feedback. A JSS of 90%+ is excellent, and below 79% makes winning work hard. — [Upwork: improve JSS](https://www.upwork.com/resources/how-to-improve-job-success-score); [Upwork Help](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/211068358)
- A third-party claim that JSS is recomputed every two weeks over 3/6/12/24-month windows, using the best one, is unverified. — [Millo](https://millo.co/upwork-job-success-score)
- Forum reports say private feedback can drive JSS even when the public rating looks fine. One user says Upwork support confirmed this. Feedback is left when a contract closes. — [Upwork Community](https://community.upwork.com/t5/Freelancers/Private-Feedback-s-Effect-on-JSS-and-the-Negative-Non/m-p/658016); [Upwork Community](https://community.upwork.com/t5/Freelancers/Private-Feedback/m-p/601491) (anecdote)
- Ethical ask: one short message after final delivery asking the client to close out the contract, not asking for praise. Avoid repeated nudges. The vendor claims that pressuring for 5 stars breaks Upwork's rules, but I could not verify this in an official source. — [GigRadar](https://gigradar.io/blog/how-to-ask-upwork-clients-for-feedback)
- **Rising Talent badge:** either an invitation from Upwork, or a 4.8+ average rating with ≥$250 earned in the past 12 months. Other requirements: a 100% complete profile, a proposal or work in the last 90 days, a JSS of ≥90% if you have one, no account holds, no negative feedback, and a verified identity. It can be earned before a JSS. — [Upwork Help/Raenest snippet](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/360049702614); [Raenest badges](https://www.raenest.com/es-ar/blog/upwork-badges)
- Upwork's 2026 guide: do quality work, communicate regularly, ask clients for feedback and anticipate their needs. "Even one strong relationship can lead to repeat projects and referrals." — [Upwork: become a freelancer 2026](https://upwork.com/resources/how-to-become-a-freelancer)
- Pallais found that *detailed* public evaluations improved new workers' later outcomes beyond simply being hired. — [NBER w18917](https://www.nber.org/papers/w18917)

### Inferences
- **JSS risk management for a newcomer:**
  - Avoid contracts you might not deliver on time. The context study found 1★ reviews for delays among Nigerian Roblox freelancers.
  - Close finished contracts promptly instead of leaving them open.
  - Keep scope tiny on the first jobs.
  - Fake reviews (the $5 "full game" pattern in the context study) are a suspension risk. Do not use them.
- **Repeat-work pattern:** after delivery, suggest the next logical item, such as map → scripting → monetization. One Nigerian freelancer in the context study (Niniola) used this with one client.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch Upwork's official JSS calculation page. How long-open contracts and contracts without feedback affect JSS is reported only on forums.

## 5. Project Catalog, consultations, invites; Africa/Nigeria-specific success

### Takeaway
Project Catalog lets you list up to 20 fixed-scope offers. Forum evidence says sales for new sellers are rare and scam-heavy, so treat Catalog as a supplement, not the main channel. Evidence specific to Nigeria is thin. One documented Nigerian game-dev case (Emmanuel Omene, Unity, Top Rated) is from 2018. Account-verification problems, such as name mismatches and VPN or location flags, are the main Nigeria-specific risk raised in 2025-era posts.

### Cited Findings
- Up to 20 Project Catalog projects at once. Upwork advises browsing existing projects, offering something more specialized, and picking work you can market with samples. — [Upwork Help](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/360057397533)
- In a 2023 community thread, a freelancer with 340 jobs over 5 years did not recall a direct Catalog sale. Another, with two projects listed, said the buyers were mostly scammers and Catalog earnings were $0. — [Upwork Community](https://community.upwork.com/t5/Freelancers/Project-Catalog-Sales-Generate-Expert-needs/m-p/1266781) (anecdote)
- **Nigerian game developer case:** Emmanuel Omene, a Unity developer, had a Top Rated profile with 92% JSS, 49 jobs and 1,685 hours (Techpoint, 2018). His background: built a 3D runner "Sango Run", worked at Gamsole, a Nigerian mobile studio, after NYSC in 2015, and later at Vericore. The snippets did not show how he got his first Upwork clients. — [Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/feature/emmanuel-omene-upwork-freelancer/) (old; snippet only)
- An older TechCabal guide (2022) notes Upwork's cons for Nigerians: fees and the cost of bidding. — [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2022/08/18/top-5-freelance-platforms-to-sell-your-tech-skills-in-nigeria/). TechCabal's 2025 coverage focuses on new tax rules for freelancers and remote workers; I saw headlines only. — [TechCabal tag](https://techcabal.com/tag/remote-workers)
- Verification problems (anecdotes):
  - A LinkedIn post reports suspensions after verifying with a NIN certificate and advises using an international passport whose name exactly matches the profile.
  - VPNs, frequent location changes and shared networks can trigger "unusual activity" flags and re-verification.

  None of this is confirmed by Upwork. — [LinkedIn](https://de.linkedin.com/in/zsolt-farnas); [send.win blog](https://blog.send.win/?p=8236); [websitebuilderinsider](https://www.websitebuilderinsider.com/can-upwork-detect-vpn/)
- Market context: about $4B spent by clients and 785K active clients in 2025, per Raenest. — [Raenest](https://www.raenest.com/blog/how-to-get-started-on-upwork)

### Inferences
- The Nigerian Roblox freelancers who earn in the context study rely on proposals, hourly work, teaching and narrow niches, with Catalog as a small extra (Adejuwon, Ilesanmi). That fits the forum view that Catalog rarely drives first sales. A few cheap, specific Catalog items, such as "Roblox leaderboard + DataStore save system", cost nothing to list and can help get a first review.
- For Nigeria specifically: complete identity verification with documents whose names match exactly, avoid VPNs, and log in from a consistent location. Losing an account is a bigger risk than any pricing mistake (inference from anecdotes).
- I found no evidence about how well invites or consultations convert for newcomers. Invites usually follow profile visibility, which follows reviews and badges such as Rising Talent.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 TechCabal or Techpoint data on Nigerian Upwork success rates. The full Techpoint article could not be fetched.
- No official Upwork policy on NIN acceptance or VPNs.
- No data on how well consultations or invites convert for new freelancers.

## 6. Common mistakes that kill new accounts

### Takeaway
The mistakes with documented backing:
- generic or AI-sounding proposals
- spending Connects on stale or crowded jobs
- taking work or payment off-platform, or falling for scams
- fake or traded reviews
- identity and verification mismatches, or VPN use
- missed deadlines that lead to bad private feedback and a low JSS
- a broad, unfocused profile

### Cited Findings
- Generic openings and copy-paste proposals perform far worse. — [Snipework](https://snipework.com/blog/upwork-cover-letter-sample); [GigUp](https://giguphq.com/blog/win-rate-on-upwork-2026) (vendor)
- Applying to jobs older than 72 hours or with more than 25 proposals is listed as a targeting mistake. — [GigUp](https://giguphq.com/blog/win-rate-on-upwork-2026) (vendor)
- Off-platform payments are not protected and cannot be recovered. — [Upwork Scams FAQ](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/34385274054547-Scams-Frequently-Asked-Questions)
- Suspension triggers listed by blogs: multiple accounts, profile-integrity violations, mismatched or unverifiable documents, VPN or location anomalies, and a low JSS. Appeals after a suspension rarely succeed (anecdote). — [GoLance](https://golance.com/blogs/upwork-account-suspended-what-to-do-next); [send.win](https://blog.send.win/?p=8236)
- A JSS below 79% makes winning work difficult. — [Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/resources/how-to-improve-job-success-score)
- A broad positioning attracts the wrong clients, and a narrow niche reduces competition. — [UpworkAlerts](https://upworkalerts.com/blog/upwork-tips-for-beginners) (vendor)
- Novices often refuse to bid below budget out of a mistaken belief that it signals low quality. Evidence says this costs them hires. — [IGC](https://www.theigc.org/publications/cutting-wages-break-how-information-gaps-hold-back-workers-online-gig-economy)

### Inferences
- Two more mistakes show up in the context study: overpromising "full game" scope at a tiny budget, which led to 1★ reviews for delays, and having strong profile copy but no visible demos.

### Gaps
- No Upwork data on why new accounts become inactive or get suspended. Everything above is vendor content or anecdote.
