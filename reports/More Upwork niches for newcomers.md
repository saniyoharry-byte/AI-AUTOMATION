# Security reviews and scrapers sharpen the automation bet

**Bottom line:** a second live Upwork pull (October 25, 2026) covered about 30 niches that the October 24 study did not rank. None of them displaces that study's leaders: AI UGC video ads, appointment/DM setting, n8n/AI automation fixes, short-form video, Shopify fixes, Sheets/Apps Script and vibe-coded app rescue. Several of the new niches are realistic for a no-badge, zero-job seller. In order, they are **Notion/Airtable/ClickUp systems** (a Lagos CS graduate went from a **$15 first job on Aug 4 to a $650 build and a repeat contract on Oct 7**, and 9 of the top 25 search results were Nigerian), **Supabase/Firebase security reviews and fixes** (a no-badge Moroccan engineer won about **$10K of "make AI-built apps safe to launch" work from June to October**, including a **$1,500 security review on Oct 15**), **web scraping** (≈4.7 posts/day, only 2 of 20 posts at 50+ proposals, but newcomer pay of **$3–10/hr**), **Etsy shop SEO plus Canva digital products**, and **Blender work paired with Python/ComfyUI automation**. For a Nigerian, **Yoruba/Igbo/Hausa AI voice-data and transcription work** is the most distinctive opening: a **$605 Yoruba voice-data job with 10–15 proposals and no JSS, hours or earnings minimum** was live on Oct 23. Most bot, extension, design and media niches are thinner, badge-saturated or pay $6–10/hr. For **Harry**, the new data **strengthens rather than changes** the October 24 recommendation of one "AI automation & integration developer" profile. It adds two concrete entry products, **a Supabase row-level-security (RLS) review** and **scraper → n8n/Sheets data pipelines**, plus MCP/Claude connector builds as a keyword. **AI-coding-agent evaluation** ($20–30/hr) and **Nigerian-language voice data** are worth a few bids as side income. Notion/ClickUp, Blender and Telegram/Discord bots should stay out of the headline.

## A weekend snapshot with four known distortions

Everything labeled **Observed** comes from read-only Upwork API pulls (MCP connector) on **October 25, 2026, about 23:00 UTC through 00:30 UTC on October 26**. The pulls covered job searches by title keyword sorted by recency (usually the newest 10 posts per term), freelancer searches (top 25 by default relevance) and full profile pulls with contract dates, amounts and review text. **Inference** marks analysis. Four distortions matter.

**It is one weekend.** The 48-hour windows cover Saturday–Sunday, October 24–25, so weekday volume is probably higher, and no trend can be read from it.

**Matching is by title only.** Bot, scraping and backend-fix work often hides under titles like "Automation Developer" or "Fix my app". Thin niches (Discord, Telegram, Chrome extensions, Firebase) are therefore undercounted the most. Velocity is estimated from the time the newest 10 posts span.

**The search job-count field is wrong, again.** Shoukat A. showed "1 completed job" in search but has 41 jobs on his profile ([profile](https://www.upwork.com/freelancers/~01959e54bbebc3c986)). Philips E. showed 1 but has 13 ([profile](https://www.upwork.com/freelancers/~01d579a362a4a0ada2)). Dmytro Z. showed 5 but has 116 contracts ([profile](https://www.upwork.com/freelancers/~016cf0e4c29dd10a2b)). Muhammad S. showed 1 but has 66 ([profile](https://www.upwork.com/freelancers/~01940baf1212127e70)). "Newcomer" claims below therefore rest on contract dates in full profiles. The API also never exposes Rising Talent, so "no badge" means no Top Rated, Top Rated Plus or Expert-Vetted. Proposal counts at the time of hire, invites and proposal texts are not visible. Several calls were throttled ("restricted" errors) and succeeded on retry.

**Web sources were read only through search snippets.** Every WebFetch call failed with DNS errors. Fiverr's trend indexes measure *search growth*, not orders, and no newcomer win-rate data by niche exists anywhere.

All job and profile text is self-written by third parties. Revenue, experience and employer claims inside it are unverified.

### Developer niches: security reviews pay, scraping wins, bots are too thin

**Supabase security work is the highest-value new finding.** **Observed:** Supabase-titled posts ran at about 3.3 a day, and 7 of the 10 newest were expert level. Several were plain security engagements: "Independent Security & Architecture Review – Deno / Supabase AI Application" at **$530** with 15–20 proposals ([job](https://www.upwork.com/jobs/~022114459476598170731)), and "Senior PostgreSQL / Supabase Security Engineer" to validate RLS on a patch, at **$300** ([job](https://www.upwork.com/jobs/~022114147352743007122)). A Firebase post read "Most of it was built with Claude Code" ($300, 50+) ([job](https://www.upwork.com/jobs/~022112188086615645444)). Another job wanted an **"MCP Server Developer to Connect Claude to Our Supabase and Notion Data"** (50+ proposals; client rated 5.0 over 41 reviews) ([job](https://www.upwork.com/jobs/~022114376406569468202)).

Search was almost fully badged (0 of 25 for "Supabase Firebase developer"), yet the clearest no-badge success of the whole pull is here. **Younes M.** (Morocco, $45/hr, ex-Oracle) has 7 fixed jobs worth $10K+ since June 2026. They include a $1,860 PR review ("he doesn't just implement something because I've asked for it — he thinks about the wider impact, flags potential issues"), a **$1,500 "Personal Trainer App security review" on Oct 15** and a $175 weekly Supabase architecture review on Oct 20. His overview opens: "I take SaaS apps that were built fast with Lovable, Cursor, Bolt or a rushed MVP and make them safe to launch" ([profile](https://www.upwork.com/freelancers/~0104b3e116989a726d)). **Michael B.** (Germany, $40/hr, no badge) sells "I test with two normal accounts, prove every gap, fix it and re-test". He shows **two sample RLS reviews** as portfolio items and won a "Software Auditor of SaaS Platform" contract on Oct 23 ([profile](https://www.upwork.com/freelancers/~0111f48c26147de4f5)).

**Inference:** sample audit write-ups substitute for reviews here. Younes's corporate background probably helped, and how much of his success transfers to a junior seller is unknown.

**Web scraping is the most winnable developer niche, and the cheapest.** **Observed:** 20 posts arrived in about 4.25 days. Only 2 had 50+ proposals, and many were $50–150 fixed. Examples are "Web Scraping for Price Data" across 8–10 sites for $52 ([job](https://www.upwork.com/jobs/~022114352933603013020)) and a $130 lead scrape ([job](https://www.upwork.com/jobs/~022112994863044685318)). The better-paid posts asked for pipelines or prior platform experience: "Python Data Scraping Web+PDF from Public Filings" at **$20–100/hr** with 15–20 proposals ([job](https://www.upwork.com/jobs/~022114496291892509689)), and an **$2,500** ad-spy platform for an "Engineer Who Has Scraped This Before" with 10–15 proposals ([job](https://www.upwork.com/jobs/~022113869524315145015)). Sellers are in a price race: 20 of the top 25 charge ≤$10/hr ([search](https://www.upwork.com/nx/search/talent/?q=web%20scraping%20data%20extraction)). **Shoukat A.** (Pakistan, $3.33/hr, no badge) earns $10–225 per job and started two new hourly scraping contracts on Oct 21–22. His overview opens with a problem ("Majority of web scraping projects fail because anti-bot systems…") and his portfolio has 12 named-site scrapers ([profile](https://www.upwork.com/freelancers/~01959e54bbebc3c986)). **Raed O.** started on $35 scraping jobs in November 2025 and moved into bots and backends ([profile](https://www.upwork.com/freelancers/~01ae41be81a93bddc6)).

**Inference:** for a developer, scraping is a review builder. Its profit lies in PDF/ETL and anti-bot pipelines, not in lead lists.

**Mobile fixes have volume but heavy bidding. Unity has light bidding but needs shipped games.** **Observed:** Flutter and React Native together produced about 12 posts a day, but **9 of the 20 newest had 50+ proposals** ([Flutter search](https://www.upwork.com/nx/search/jobs/?q=Flutter&sort=recency)). Newcomers entered either cheaply (Nahom D. at ≈$8/hr on a $250 job that turned hourly ([profile](https://www.upwork.com/freelancers/~010979e876740b8e4f))) or with a narrow, provable offer (Michael B.'s $1,325 widget job). The one Nigerian in the top 25, **Olajire A.** (Top Rated Plus, $80K+), leads with his own voice-AI banking product in Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo and Hausa and with "rescue stalled codebases" ([profile](https://www.upwork.com/freelancers/~014f42d3e94868b33d)).

Unity had **no post at 50+** among the 10 newest, and 5 had fewer than 10 proposals ([search](https://www.upwork.com/nx/search/jobs/?q=Unity&sort=recency)). About a third of those posts are art jobs, though. **Andres R.** (Peru, no badge) won his first contract on Sep 6 and got rehired by the same client. He was on 6 jobs by Oct 21, ranking on playable builds and Google Play titles. Review: "If you can imagine a game mechanic or feature, he can build it" ([profile](https://www.upwork.com/freelancers/~01cea763edc1b3860f)).

**Bots and extensions are skill keywords, not niches.** **Observed:** "Discord bot" returned **4 jobs in 10 days and none in the last 72 hours** ([search](https://www.upwork.com/nx/search/jobs/?q=Discord%20bot&sort=recency)). ## Why winners rank and get hired, and the padding not to copy

**Observed traits of new winners in this pull** match October 24 and add some detail.

## Conclusion

The second pull changes the *shape* of the advice more than its direction. On October 24, "app rescue" was a promising but vague niche. On October 25 it had a concrete, priced product, the security/RLS review, which no-badge sellers were winning on the strength of sample write-ups rather than reviews. Scraping adds a high-volume, low-competition way to earn reviews inside the same automation story. Both leave Harry with one coherent profile in which every new skill points at the same buyer: someone with a half-built system that is broken, leaking or slow.

The broader lesson is that Nigerian advantage on Upwork shows up in two very different places. One is native-language AI data, where the nationality itself is the qualification and the pay is modest. The other is technical work buyers can verify, where Nigerians already rank (Olajire A., Philips E., Hamza M.) but only behind checkable proof. A newcomer who claims "AI Trainer" or "RLHF" without that proof joins the most crowded and least-hired group in the data. That is the clearest sign that proof assets, not keywords, decide who gets hired.
